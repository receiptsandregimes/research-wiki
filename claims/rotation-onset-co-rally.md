# Claim

Frank: at the **beginning** of capital rotation events, gold and the S&P 500
rise together — the regime switch starts with both bid, before they diverge.
Examples he gives: the 2000s (gold bull coexisting with mid-2000s equity
highs), and gold breaking its peg around the London Gold Pool collapse (record
says March 1968; Frank said "late 1968" — date to verify) while the SPX was at
all-time highs ("imagine if there was no peg how it would have been screaming").

- `origin: frank` — recorded faithfully; independently re-research before use.
- Why it might be true: rotation onsets are usually liquidity/reflation
  events (the thing capital rotates OUT of — cash, bonds, a pegged FX regime —
  is what both assets are priced against); early in the shift both nominal
  assets bid together before relative-strength regimes sort them out.
- Why to be skeptical: post-hoc window picking; "beginning of a rotation event"
  is a label applied after the fact, so the test needs a forward-looking
  definition (e.g. months where BOTH assets break their own 12-month highs
  within the same quarter) rather than pre-chosen episodes.
- Test (to propose): forward-looking screen 1971+: months where both assets
  post trailing-3-mo highs simultaneously; measure how often gold-only,
  SPX-only, and both rise over the following 6/12 months, and quantify the
  tax a one-of-two [[gold-spx-rs-ratio-rotation]] rule pays in co-move months
  vs holding both. This is the direct test of whether relative momentum gives
  up the first leg of every regime shift.
