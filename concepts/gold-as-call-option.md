# Gold as a call option

The heuristic (held by [[frank]], [[jay-kaeppel]], and a long lineage of gold
advocates): gold's payoff is convex — it bleeds premium-like losses through
long chop decades (1980–2000: ~20 years to return a 1980 nominal high) and
pays violently in monetary/debasement regimes (1971–79, 2001–11, 2024–26).
Holding gold ≈ paying premium; the regime breaks ≈ expiry into a real move.

## Frank's refinement (see [[gold-call-option-filtered-levered]])

The bleed is tolerable-to-profitable if the position is **momentum-filtered**
(kill the filter during chop = stop paying premium in the chop) and the
filtered exposure is **levered** (express the convexity with size on the
trend-positive subset only). The filter converts the option analogy from
"hold and bleed" to "buy the option only when it's going to be in the money" —
which is precisely absolute momentum applied to a single asset.

## The live contradiction (unresolved, recorded 2026-10-08)

The same thesis about *what gold is* supports opposite entry engines:

- **Momentum entry** (Frank, this wiki's frame): convexity means you only need
  to be holding during the regime breaks; a trend filter finds them.
- **Contrarian/cheap entry** ([[jay-kaeppel]]'s public discipline, per his
  Sentimentrader work): buy after multi-year lows / pessimist sentiment —
  because the bleed is premium, cheapen the premium by buying at the bottom of
  the bear.

Both can be partially right (momentum for timing, cheapness for sizing), and
the tension is testable: same asset, two entries, 1971+, compare worst-5-year
outcomes and capture of the three bull regimes. Filed rather than smoothed
over; per SCHEMA.md update policy this stays `contested: true` until a study
verdict.
